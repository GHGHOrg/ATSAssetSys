Markers

  1. Portfolio chooser at the top: a dropdown of the portfolios in the order they were created. It opens on the portfolio you came from, or on the first portfolio when opened from More. Changing it redraws everything below; the choice is not saved. A back arrow returns to where you came from. The "Retirement" entry is illustrative only.
  2. Portfolio value, with the age of the oldest price used in this portfolio (4.8; a holding valued at cost with "no price" is not counted). While a refresh runs, an "updating" indicator shows on the price part only. A quiet line shows only when a refresh failed, with no pop-up.
  3. Four breakdowns stacked in this order: asset type, holding, sector, geography. Each has its own donut and rows, under the same rules as Net Worth (Total): a gap between slices, a percentage on a slice of 5% or more, a row percentage only for a slice under 5%, and a TalkBack label on every row that reads the percentage in full. The base is the portfolio value, so each breakdown adds up to 100.0%. There is no cash and no breakdown by portfolio (those are on Net Worth (Total)). Asset type always lists stocks and ETFs, even at 0.0%; the other breakdowns list only what has value. Sector and geography show an "Unclassified" slice for holdings with no value (C14).
  4. With no portfolio the screen says "No portfolios yet" with a Create a portfolio button.
  5. A portfolio with no holdings shows $0.00 and one plain line in place of the four breakdowns, because every share of $0.00 is undefined (new rule, see the spec.md hunk).

A slice that fills the whole ring (100.0%) is drawn with no gap. The percentage on it follows the same rule as any slice of 5% or more.
